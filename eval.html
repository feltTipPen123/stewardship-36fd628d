If it can't be measured, it can't be handed over.

Bolter's promise is "give a responsibility to an anima and it just works." That's a claim about trust. This is how we put a number on it — borrowing the best of the agent-eval world and adding the parts no one has built.

See the design → What we borrow

⚡ The whole thing in 30 seconds

"Offloadable" isn't a task score — it's a property of the (person, anima(s), responsibility) triple measured over calendar time. So the eval simulates time, events, a principal with limited attention, and traps. We borrow the proven mechanics — programmatic state-checks, pass^k reliability, checkpoint credit, escalation-F1 — and add what nobody has: multi-day persistence, re-grounding after context loss, an attention budget, and a team owning one duty. The output isn't a number; it's a granted autonomy level per responsibility.

01

Why no existing benchmark fits

// "offloadable" is a property of a relationship over time, not a task result

Every public agent benchmark collapses at least one axis that matters for handing over a standing responsibility. The mechanics are borrowable; the frame is what we have to build.

⏳
Axis #1 — they're single-sessionτ-bench, GAIA, SWE-bench: one conversation, one goal, one sitting. No days with idle gaps, no "wake on an event next Tuesday."
🧍
Axis #2 — the "human" is a strangerThe simulated user is a momentary customer, not a principal you're accountable to who has limited attention and will be annoyed if you over-ask.
🤝
Axis #3 — they score the agent, never the handoffThe real question — can a person safely rely on this? — is about trust calibration in the pair. No agent benchmark measures it.

The closest long-horizon evals that exist (Vending-Bench, Anthropic's Project Vend) measure profit over a long run, not "did it serve the delegator's evolving intent and know when to ask for help." So we borrow heavily for the mechanics — and treat time, a principal, traps, and a team as the novel core.

03

The gap nobody fills

// why we can't just adopt one benchmark — these are net-new

Across 50+ sources, five things that decide real offloadability are measured by nothing. These are the parts Offload-Bench has to invent.

Calendar-time with idle gaps

Everything tops out at hours-in-one-sitting or token-count proxies. None model real days where nothing happens, then an event requires waking, re-orienting, and acting.

Re-grounding after context loss

Memory benchmarks test retrieval. None score the actual failure: resuming a half-done duty after a context reset and correctly reconstructing "where I left off and what's still owed."

Escalation vs. an attention budget

HiL-Bench's "human" always answers. None score escalation frequency against a busy principal you must not over-ping — the calibration that makes hands-off bearable.

A team owning one duty

Multi-agent benchmarks score teams solving tasks. None score N animas jointly owning a standing duty: handoffs, shared memory, who's accountable when one drops it.

Self-detected drift

The Vending-Bench "meltdown" is observed from outside. Nothing scores whether the agent notices its own decay over a long tenure and recovers or flags it.

04

Offload-Bench: the design

// reuse-first — rides bolter_qa_run + the scheduler, not a new engine

Built on what bolter already has. It runs through bolter_qa_run({ target, taskIds }) (the QA-framework-as-platform direction) and the existing bolter-vs-cc-bench rig. The one new primitive: a virtual clock so days compress to minutes and drive the scheduler — today it's wall-clock node-cron (Atlas, Stop 03), which is exactly why we make the clock injectable.

The unit — a Responsibility Scenario

action-requiredquiet / noise → don't spamambiguous → should escalateirreversible → ask firstmanipulation → resistpreference stated → persist

↻
Why a timeline, not a promptThe duty is run as a sequence of clock ticks and injected events — including quiet ticks (where the right move is silence) and traps — so we measure behaviour over tenure, not a single clever answer.
05

The score is a profile, not a number

// a single headline number is exactly what hides unreliability

Nine dimensions, each borrowed from a proven source, each catching a distinct delegation failure. They roll up to a granted autonomy level — they don't average into one figure.

1
τ-bench · WorkBench

Acceptance

Programmatic state-check: did the duty actually get discharged against the charter's criteria.

Catches: looks busy, achieves nothing.

2
τ-bench (pass^k)

Reliability

Run each scenario k times; report pass^k and the pass@k−pass^k gap, plus worst-of-N.

Catches: succeeds sometimes — the luck gap.

3
TheAgentCompany

Checkpoint credit

Partial credit across the timeline's checkpoints, with a bonus for full discharge.

Catches: binary scoring hiding near-misses.

4
HiL-Bench + AV reports

Escalation-F1

Asked at the right moments, within the attention budget — false vs. missed escalation.

Catches: over-pinging, or silently overstepping.

5
novel (the gap)

Re-grounding

After the forced reset: does it reconstruct "where I left off / what's owed" correctly.

Catches: restarting from zero; broken promises.

6
Multi-IF · π-Bench

Instruction half-life

Adherence to standing instructions & stated preferences across the whole timeline.

Catches: forgetting the rules over time.

7
Holistic Agent Leaderboard

Cost & intervention

Task-success-per-dollar and actions-per-intervention against the budget caps.

Catches: right answers that aren't economically viable.

8
WebGuard · Project Vend

Safety

Irreversible-action containment, confident-wrong rate, manipulability under pressure.

Catches: confident irreversible mistakes; pushover.

9
Vending-Bench

Drift / meltdown

Catastrophic-failure rate over long runs, and whether decay is self-detected.

Catches: silent spirals over a long tenure.

06

The deliverable: a granted autonomy level

// per responsibility-type — the actual answer to "can I hand this over?"

The profile gates an anima to a level on an SAE-style ladder (after Feng et al. / the Cloud Security Alliance), per responsibility-type, with a capability-control matrix that forbids high autonomy over irreversible/high-impact actions until the safety + reliability bars are cleared.

L1

Suggests

Drafts; the human does everything. Human-in-the-loop.

L2

Acts on approval

Proposes each action; human approves before it fires. Pre-action oversight.

L3

Acts, escalates on boundary

Handles the routine; asks at ambiguous / irreversible edges.

L4

Acts, human monitors

Runs the duty; human watches and can intervene. Human-on-the-loop.

L5

Acts, reports after

Owns it; consults the human after the fact. Human-out-of-the-loop.

L6

Fully autonomous

No routine human involvement. Earned only by the highest profiles, narrow scopes.

🎚️
What "it just works" actually meansNot "L6 everywhere" — it means each anima carries an honest, evidence-backed level per duty, so a person knows exactly how much they can stop watching.
07

Three modes

// one anima · a team · and the pair with a real human

① Single-anima

The baseline. One anima tends one Responsibility Scenario end-to-end across the timeline. Establishes the per-duty profile and autonomy level.

② Multi-anima (team owns one duty)

The same duty split across N animas. Adds team KPIs (MultiAgentBench-style): did the duty stay discharged when one dropped it, consistency across the team, clean handoffs.

③ Human-pair arm (highest fidelity)

Real users, with seeded anima errors, measuring over-/under-trust (Lee & See; Parasuraman & Riley). The only mode that answers "can a person offload safely" — because that's a property of the pair.

!
The point of mode ③An anima can be perfect and still un-offloadable if the human can't calibrate when to trust it. Delegation success is measured on the pair, not the model alone.

09

How to build it, cheap first

// a baseline in two weeks, a real bench in a quarter

P0

Baseline (~1–2 weeks)

6–8 hand-authored scenarios, virtual clock, programmatic oracles + one LLM-judge dimension, pass^k = 5, on the existing harness. Score today's self-scheduling anima — establish the "before."

reuse bolter_qa_run · bolter-vs-cc-bench rig

P1

Delegation dimensions

Add escalation-F1 against an attention budget, the forced-reset re-grounding test, and cost / intervention budgets. Now it measures the things task benchmarks can't.

escalation-F1 · re-grounding · budgets

P2

Scale & the human arm

A scenario generator + holdout set; the multi-agent (team-owns-one-duty) mode; and the human-pair arm for trust calibration with real users.

generated scenarios · multi-agent · human-pair

★
Lead with these threepass^k, the 80% time-horizon, and escalation-F1 — grade each anima to an autonomy level per responsibility-type, and report the profile, never a single number.
