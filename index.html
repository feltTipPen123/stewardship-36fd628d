An anima shouldn't babysit a timer. It should hold a responsibility the way a good steward does — with a charter, a memory, and the judgment to know when it's done and when to ask.

Jump to the proposal → Why today's model breaks

⚡ The whole thing in 30 seconds

Today an anima owns a responsibility by self-scheduling a timer and re-running a loop. That fights two laws of nature at once: AI quality decays as context grows, and a loop never tells you whether you're making progress. The fix isn't a smarter loop — it's a durable, verifiable, event-driven object the anima tends: a charter, a compact memory, signal-driven wakeups, and a fresh-eyes check on progress. So it just works — and knows when to bother you.

01

How an anima holds a responsibility today

// the self-scheduling loop, as it actually works in bolter

When you hand an anima a standing job — "give me a morning brief", "watch this deal", "keep the roadmap tidy" — it creates a scheduled_tasks row with a cron string or a one-time runAt. A clock fires it. The anima wakes, does a turn, posts, and goes quiet until the next tick.

⚙️
In one lineThe clock owns the anima. The anima owns a cron string. Nobody owns the outcome.

That's elegant, and it shipped. But look closely at what each fire actually has to work with, and three structural gaps appear — confirmed straight from the code:

◢ what a fire gets today

  • A one-line description ("Morning brief — calendar + priorities").
  • A lastRunAt timestamp.
  • Whatever chat history survived the context window.
  • mustDeliverVisibleReply — it must post something.

◢ what it does NOT have

  • No definition of success. No acceptance criteria, no "done".
  • No durable state. Checkpoints were removed; nextRunAt is never even populated.
  • No memory of the last fire beyond chat that may have been pruned.
  • No way to say "wake me when X happens" — only the clock.

So a cron responsibility fires forever. The anima re-derives "where are we?" from decaying chat, grades its own work against a vague sentence, and loops. For a five-minute reminder that's fine. For the thing Bolter actually promises — take this off my plate for weeks — it's standing on two fault lines.

▸ Deep dive: the exact mechanism from the codebase

Trigger: an in-process scheduler (scheduler.ts) registers node-cron jobs and setTimeout timers from scheduled_tasks. On fire it enqueues a durable dispatch_jobs row (idempotency-keyed) — it does not call the anima directly.

Pickup: a worker claims the job (FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED) and runs runScheduledJob → runAnimaTurn with the description as a "Scheduler" system message, executionClassOverride: 'heavy_tools', and no decline_response.

Self-scheduling tools: bolter_schedule_task / bolter_update_task / bolter_cancel_task / bolter_list_tasks. The anima can refine its cron or runAt, but cannot say "sleep until this event" or "wake me in 5 min" without an explicit re-schedule.

Two parallel notions of "task": scheduled_tasks (recurring duties) and anima_tasks (user-facing multi-dispatch work, with a real status enum) — plus a vestigial tasks dispatch-tracker whose checkpointCount/progress fields are no longer written. Progress lives on work, never on the duty.

02

The two forces the loop is fighting

// neither is a bug you can patch — they're properties of the medium

The self-scheduling loop isn't badly built. It's built against physics. Two well-documented effects push directly against "an AI tends a job reliably for weeks," and a loop happens to maximize both of them.

Force #1 — The decay function

An AI gets worse as its context grows — long before the window is "full." The advertised context window is not the usable one. And in a long autonomous run, errors don't just happen — they compound.

Why a loop makes it worseA self-scheduled loop accumulates context and lengthens the trajectory — the exact two variables the decay function punishes. Bigger model windows don't save you: it's a property of attention, not a quota.
▸ Deep dive: why quality decays with length mechanism

Lost in the middle. Models show a U-shaped attention curve — strong at the start and end of context, weak in the middle — and the dip deepens as you add material. With the answer mid-context, GPT-3.5's multi-doc QA once fell below its no-document baseline: the right context actively hurt.1

Context rot. Chroma tested 18 frontier models and found every one degrades as input grows, even on trivial tasks; semantically-similar distractors compound the loss; models do better on focused prompts than full ones.3 RULER and HELMET show the real usable size is far below the advertised one, and simple needle-in-haystack demos badly overstate it.5,6

The math of compounding. With constant per-step accuracy p and no self-correction, the horizon you can reach before failure is H_s(p) = ⌈ln(s)/ln(p)⌉ — so small step-accuracy gains buy exponential horizon. The lever is fewer, more reliable steps.22

Agentic accumulation. Drew Breunig's taxonomy: poisoning (a hallucination re-cited as fact — DeepMind's Gemini-plays-Pokémon fixating on impossible goals), distraction (the model parrots its own history instead of planning), confusion (too many tools/irrelevant content), clash (new info conflicts with old).15,17,18

Force #2 — A loop can't tell you it's working

A timer encodes when to act. It says nothing about whether the job is getting done. Without an external definition of success, an agent asked to judge itself defaults to the most expensive possible answer.

🔁
The AutoGPT lesson, in one sentenceAn agent grading its own vague goal "consistently found ways to improve completed work, with no concept of 'good enough'" — so it never stops.7,8

This is the documented failure of the whole AutoGPT/BabyAGI generation, and it's a termination-design failure, not an intelligence failure. Three traps, all present in a bare cron loop:

◢ self-grading is biased

  • Models rate their own outputs higher (self-enhancement bias), and judging your own narration is gameable — reword the reasoning, "improve" the score.10,11
  • An agent optimizing a vague spec drifts toward whatever is easiest to declare done — reward hacking, in-context.21,23

◢ "done" has no meaning here

  • A cron task has no success field and no completion condition. It fires until a human disables it.
  • No no-progress detection: the same situation produces the same action, forever, with nobody noticing.
✓
So the goal becomesMake a responsibility carry its own definition of success, judge progress with fresh eyes, and give the anima the right to stop — or to ask.
03

What the field already figured out

// the patterns that survive contact with production — skim the cards, open what you want

Every credible long-running agent system has converged on a similar playbook. None of it is exotic. Here are the six ideas we're building on — collapsed by default so you can take only what you need.

▸ 1 · Wake on signals, not on a timer scheduling

Event-driven activation beats polling by 70–90% on latency and — more importantly — gives zero wasted runs: the agent runs only when there's real work. The mature pattern is three triggers: events (react to change), cron (genuinely periodic work like a daily brief), and a low-frequency heartbeat that is a reasoning gate — "is anything worth doing?" — not a blind act.23,26,27 The classic agent models say the same: BDI separates deliberation from execution and re-deliberates on events, not on a clock.28

▸ 2 · Externalize memory; re-ground every wake memory

Generative Agents (Stanford "Smallville") keep a memory stream retrieved by recency × importance × relevance, plus periodic reflection into higher-level notes.6 MemGPT/Letta treat memory like an OS — core/recall/archival tiers the agent self-edits.20,41 Anthropic's harness re-grounds on a fixed startup routine every session (read progress files + logs, consult the feature list, verify the environment, then act) and uses a persistent memory tool (+39% on internal evals vs. baseline).13,25,43,46 The rule: trust durable notes, never accumulated in-context narration.

▸ 3 · Recitation — keep the goal in recent context context engineering

Manus rewrites a todo.md every step, which recites the objective into the end of the context — exactly where attention is strongest — to fight lost-in-the-middle and goal drift across 50-tool-call tasks.10 Claude Code's internal TodoWrite is the same trick. Cheap, model-agnostic, high-leverage.6b

▸ 4 · Judge progress against criteria — with a fresh lens verification

Don't let the agent grade its own homework. Prefer verifiable completion (an external check) over declared completion ("I'm done").18 Anthropic's harness encodes this as a feature list with a passes boolean and an explicit "it is unacceptable to edit the tests."46 Cognition's code-review loop runs in completely clean context and catches ~58% of severe bugs because it avoids the author's biases.4 Use a different lens/family, and judge actions and state deltas, not the agent's prose.10,13

▸ 5 · Design termination explicitly stopping

"Agent design is termination design." Four stop classes you should implement all of: goal-based (verifiable, not declared), resource budgets (max beats / tokens / cost), pathology (result-aware no-progress + loop + confidence-collapse detection), and external (user cancel, circuit breaker).18,19b The honest framing is the halting problem — stopping conditions are the pragmatic answer to fundamental uncertainty.

▸ 6 · Durable, dormant, resumable — and accumulate across runs durability

Model a long responsibility as a durable workflow that hibernates and wakes on a signal/timer, checkpointing after each step, with idempotent side effects — not a live while(!done) process.30,31 Cloudflare's Durable-Objects pattern (hibernate at zero cost, wake on alarm, reconstitute from storage) maps almost 1:1 onto a per-user anima.13a,22 And Devin's lesson: each scheduled run should build on the last, not start cold — "the longer it runs, the more useful its output, because it's building on a growing body of its own context."18b

One caveat from the same literature: parallelize reads, serialize writes. Multi-agent fan-out wins for read-only investigation (Anthropic: +90% on research) but conflicting parallel decisions produce unmergeable output (Cognition's "Don't Build Multi-Agents").2,3,33,35

04

The proposal: Stewardship

// reframe a responsibility from "a recurring alarm" to "a durable object an anima tends"

One idea, six pillars. A responsibility stops being a cron string and becomes a small, durable object with a charter, a memory, triggers, and a verdict. The anima doesn't loop over it — it tends it: wakes when there's reason to, re-grounds from durable state, does a short beat, has its progress checked by fresh eyes, and decides when to act next or to ask you.

🌱
The reframeA beat replaces a tick: short, small-context, re-grounded, assessed, and logged — so each one is reliable and each one builds on the last.
1
fixes self-grading + drift

A charter, not a cron string

Each responsibility carries intent (what success looks like, in the owner's words + the anima's restatement), acceptance criteria (verifiable signals), and explicit scope & guardrails. Written once, recited at the top of every beat.

Kills: grading a vague sentence; goal drift over weeks.

2
fixes context rot + amnesia

Durable working state, not chat archaeology

Every responsibility owns a compact "where things stand" record the anima maintains, plus an append-only ledger of beats. At wake it re-grounds from this — small and high-signal — not from chat that's been pruned. Restorable compression: keep the pointer, drop the bulk.

Kills: the decay function's first variable — unbounded accumulating context.

3
fixes the polling anti-pattern

Wake on signals, not just the clock

First-class event triggers — inbound mail, calendar change, a peer anima finishing, a threshold crossed, the owner replying. Dormant until signaled. Cron only for genuinely periodic beats; a low-frequency heartbeat that reasons ("anything worth doing?") before it acts.

Kills: dispatches burned on "nothing happened"; doom-looping on a timer.

4
fixes "done" + self-bias

Progress judged by a fresh lens

Every beat that mutates or claims completion ends with an assessment in clean context against the acceptance criteria — a different lens, not the anima's own narration. Produces real states: advancing · stable · blocked · done. Result-aware no-progress and loop detection built in.

Kills: no concept of "good enough"; silent doom loops.

5
makes "just works" honest

Adaptive cadence + honest escalation

Next-wake is chosen from the assessed state within guardrails: back off when stable, lean in when active, sleep when waiting on an event, surface a crisp ask when blocked or low-confidence. Hard budgets + circuit breakers. Knowing when to bother you is part of the product.

Kills: fire-forever cron; runaway cost; silent failure.

6
fixes trajectory-length decay

Short beats · delegate reads · serialize writes

Keep each beat short and small-context to minimize both decay variables. For multi-step work the tending anima holds compact continuous context and spawns fresh-context sub-beats for parallel investigation that return distilled findings — but decisions and writes stay single-threaded.

Kills: long brittle trajectories; conflicting parallel writes.

🧭
How the six fit togetherP1 gives the beat a destination. P2 gives it grounding. P3 decides when it happens. P4 decides whether it counts. P5 decides what's next. P6 keeps each beat inside the reliable zone.
05

What this means in the codebase

// concrete, reuse-first, aggressive cutover — not a parallel system

This is deliberately an evolution of what's there, not a rewrite. It reuses the dispatch_jobs queue, the dispatch_complete waiter machinery, the memory infra, and the event sources we already capture in-process. We promote scheduled_tasks in place rather than running two schedulers.

// 1 · Promote the thin cron row into a real responsibility object // (evolve scheduled_tasks; the vestigial `tasks` checkpoint fields retire) responsibilities { id, animaId, groupId, charter: jsonb, // { intent, restatement, scope, guardrails } acceptance: jsonb, // verifiable criteria → checked by the fresh lens state: jsonb, // compact "where things stand" (the re-ground source) status: 'advancing'|'stable'|'blocked'|'done'|'retired', triggers: jsonb, // [{kind:'cron'|'event'|'heartbeat', ...}] ← P3 nextWakeAt: timestamp, // finally populated — adaptive ← P5 budget: jsonb, // { maxBeats, tokenCeiling, costCeiling } ← P5 } responsibility_beats { // append-only ledger — replaces "loop & forget" ← P2 id, responsibilityId, firedBy, summary, assessment: jsonb, // { verdict, criteriaMet[], confidence } ← P4 nextWake: jsonb, // the decision + reason } // 2 · scheduler.ts — keep cron/timer; add two paths + registerEventTrigger() // subscribe to existing in-process event sources (no dupes) + adaptiveWake() // honor responsibilities.nextWakeAt instead of forever-cron + heartbeatBeat() // low-freq reasoning gate: load state → "act or sleep?" // 3 · run-scheduled-job.ts → run-beat.ts async function runBeat(responsibilityId) { const ctx = reground(charter, state, lastK(beats)) // NOT raw chat ← P2/P3 recitation const work = doShortBeat(ctx) // small context, may fan-out reads ← P6 const verdict = assessFreshLens(work, acceptance) // clean context, diff lens ← P4 appendLedger(work, verdict); updateState(work) decideNextWake(verdict) || escalateToOwner(verdict) // ← P5 } // 4 · new model-facing tools (deprecate "fire forever + grade yourself") bolter_set_charter · bolter_update_responsibility_state · bolter_set_next_beat bolter_watch_event · bolter_escalate_to_owner // 5 · system-prompt.ts — recite at the TOP of the beat, not a passive list ## This beat → charter · acceptance criteria · compact state · last beat & verdict
♻️
Reuse, don't rebuildEvent triggers ride the existing dispatch_complete waiters and the events we already capture; the durable queue and memory infra stay. The new surface area is a richer row, a ledger table, and five tools.
06

A beat, end to end

// what replaces "the clock fires → the anima loops"

📡

A signal arrives

An event fires, a periodic beat is due, or the heartbeat's reasoning gate decides there's something worth doing. Otherwise the responsibility stays dormant — at zero cost.

event · cron · heartbeat-gate  →  enqueue one durable beat job

🧭

Re-ground from durable state

The beat opens with the charter, acceptance criteria, compact state, and the last few ledger entries recited at the top — not reconstructed from decaying chat.

small, high-signal context  ·  recitation pattern

⚒️

Do one short beat

Make incremental progress on the charter. Heavy or parallel investigation is delegated to fresh-context sub-beats that return distilled findings; the decision stays here.

minimize both decay variables  ·  serialize writes

🔍

Check progress with fresh eyes

A clean-context pass grades the beat against the acceptance criteria and watches for no-progress/loops. Verdict: advancing · stable · blocked · done.

not self-narration  ·  verifiable over declared

📓

Log & compact

Append the beat + verdict to the ledger and update the compact state. The window never grows unbounded; each run leaves the next one better-grounded.

append-only ledger  ·  restorable compression

🧠

Decide what's next — or ask

From the verdict, set the next wake adaptively (back off / lean in / sleep-on-event), or, if blocked or low-confidence or over budget, surface a crisp, specific ask to the owner. Then go dormant.

adaptive cadence  ·  honest escalation  ·  budgets & circuit breakers

07

Tradeoffs & what we're not doing

// being honest about the cost, and drawing the scope line

accept More moving parts than a cron row

A richer object + a ledger + a verifier pass is more than one timestamp. It's the minimum machinery for reliability over weeks — and it's mostly data, not new services.

bound The fresh-lens check costs tokens

Run it only on beats that mutate state or claim "done" — not on read-only or "nothing changed" beats. Cheap clean-context model, tight prompt.

watch Adaptive cadence could oscillate

Guardrails clamp min/max intervals and require a reason on every change; budgets and circuit breakers cap the blast radius if the heuristic misbehaves.

accept Aggressive cutover, not a migration

Promote scheduled_tasks in place and move animas over wholesale, rather than maintaining two scheduling systems in parallel.

not doing A generic workflow engine

This is a responsibility object on top of the existing dispatch queue — not Temporal-in-a-box. Reuse the infra we have; flag real strain as future work.

not doing Rate limits or concurrency caps

No per-anima dispatch caps, no throttling of multi-anima interaction. Intelligent routing and event-driven dormancy do the load-shaping, not artificial limits.

not doing Timing-gate "fixes"

No magic sleeps or min-delays standing in for correctness. Cadence is derived from assessed state, not tuned constants.

not doing Duplicate event sources

Event triggers subscribe to the signals we already capture in-process — no second webhook for an event we already see.

🎯
The betIf a responsibility carries its own destination, its own memory, its own sense of progress, and the right to ask — then "just works" stops being a hope and becomes a property you can point at in the ledger.
